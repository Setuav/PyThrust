    Blade-Element Propeller

    BladeElementPropeller follows the blade-element/vortex formulation of Drela's QPROP [1], with the section model of the QPROP user guide [2]. Each blade station is solved on its own; the stations' loads then sum to the thrust and torque.

    Symbols

    Symbol Description SI Unit
    \(\Omega\) Propeller angular speed, \(2\pi n\) \(\text{rad/s}\)
    \(B\) Number of blades Dimensionless
    \(R_{\text{tip}}\) Propeller tip radius \(\text{m}\)
    \(r\) Radius of a blade station \(\text{m}\)
    \(c\) Blade chord \(\text{m}\)
    \(\beta\) Blade angle from the plane of rotation \(\text{rad}\)
    \(W_a, W_t\) Axial and tangential velocity seen by the blade \(\text{m/s}\)
    \(\phi, \alpha\) Inflow angle and angle of attack \(\text{rad}\)
    \(\Gamma\) Circulation of one blade \(\text{m}^2\text{/s}\)
    \(c_l, c_d\) Section lift and drag coefficients Dimensionless
    \(Re, M\) Section Reynolds and Mach numbers Dimensionless
    \(\mu, a\) Air viscosity and speed of sound \(\text{kg/(m s)}\), \(\text{m/s}\)

    QPROP Propeller Files

    A QPROP propeller file describes the blade itself: chord and blade angle at radial stations, and the airfoil's lift and drag parameters. The format is defined in the QPROP user guide. pythrust.io.read_qprop_propeller reads one into a BladeGeometry in SI units:

    from pythrust.io import read_qprop_propeller

blade = read_qprop_propeller("prop.txt")
print(blade.blade_count, blade.radius_m)
print(blade.r_m, blade.chord_m, blade.blade_angle_rad)
print(blade.sections[0].cl_alpha_per_rad)

    The file holds, in order:

    Line Content
    1 Name
    2 Nblades [R]; without R, the last station's radius is the tip radius
    3 CL0 CL_a
    4 CLmin CLmax
    5 CD0 CD2u CD2l CLCD0
    6 REref REexp
    7 Rfac Cfac Bfac
    8 Radd Cadd Badd
    9 on r chord beta, one station per line from root to tip

    Radii and chords become value * fac + add in metres, and the blade angle beta * Bfac + Badd in degrees, converted to radians. A station line may append its own CL0 CL_a CLmin CLmax CD0 CD2u CD2l CLCD0 REref REexp; values it leaves out take those of lines 3 to 6. Lines starting with # and blank lines are skipped, and text after ! is ignored.

    CL_a is read per radian. The QPROP guide does not state its unit; its example value of 5.8 is a lift slope per radian.

    The tip radius R on line 2 is scaled like the station radii, as the QPROP guide states. QPROP 1.22 itself applies Rfac and Radd only to the stations and uses R as written, so a file that gives R with Rfac other than 1 or Radd other than 0 gives different tip losses in QPROP and in PyThrust. Without R, or with the radii in metres, the two agree. R must not be less than the last station's radius.

    UIUC Geometry Files

    The UIUC Propeller Database gives the measured geometry of many small propellers next to their wind-tunnel data. A geometry file, such as apce_10x5_geom.txt, has a header line r/R c/R beta and one station per line from root to tip: radius and chord as fractions of the tip radius, and the blade angle in degrees.

    The file gives neither the diameter, the blade count nor the airfoil, so pythrust.io.read_uiuc_geometry takes them as arguments; the one section applies to every station:

    from pythrust.io import read_uiuc_geometry
from pythrust.propeller import BladeElementPropeller, BladeSection

# The example section of the QPROP user guide, a generic starting point.
section = BladeSection(
    cl0=0.50, cl_alpha_per_rad=5.8, cl_min=-0.3, cl_max=1.2,
    cd0=0.028, cd2_upper=0.050, cd2_lower=0.020, cl_at_cd0=0.5,
    reynolds_ref=70000.0, reynolds_exp=-0.7,
)
blade = read_uiuc_geometry("apce_10x5_geom.txt", diameter_m=0.254, section=section)
propeller = BladeElementPropeller(blade)

    The section parameters set the accuracy. With the generic section above, the APC Thin Electric 10x5 gives a static thrust coefficient 7–15% above the UIUC measurement and a power coefficient 16–42% above it, the largest gap at the lowest RPM. Section parameters fitted to the propeller's airfoil narrow the gap.

    APC Geometry Files

    APC publishes a geometry file for each propeller, such as 10x5E-PERF.PE0, on its technical data page. The file gives the chord and blade angle (TWIST) at about 50 stations in inches, the tip radius, the hub transition radius, the blade count, and the two airfoils of the blade: the first up to a transition start radius, the second from a transition end radius. APC's thin electric propellers, for example, go from an Eppler E63 to the APC12, equivalent to a NACA 4412.

    pythrust.io.read_apc_geometry reads the stations from the hub transition outward. The blade angle is that of the chord line, between the leading- and trailing-edge mold parting lines; the file's TWIST column matches its LE-TE pitch, not the lower PRATHER pitch a gauge on the lower surface would read.

    sections is one section for the whole blade, or a mapping from the file's airfoil names to sections. apc_airfoil_polars() gives the polars of the airfoils APC names, E63, APC12 and CLARK-Y, computed with NeuralFoil:

    from pythrust.io import read_apc_geometry
from pythrust.propeller import BladeElementPropeller, apc_airfoil_polars

blade = read_apc_geometry("10x5E-PERF.PE0", apc_airfoil_polars())
propeller = BladeElementPropeller(blade)

    Between the two transition radii the stations mix the two airfoils: polars in a BlendedPolar, BladeSection parameters linearly.

    Against UIUC's wind-tunnel measurements of 75 APC propellers, this model is about as accurate as APC's own performance tables: some 10% mean error per propeller in thrust and power, less than the tables' in thrust. See Validation.

    Airfoil Polars

    An AirfoilPolar tabulates an airfoil's lift and drag coefficients against angle of attack and Reynolds number. AirfoilPolar.from_neuralfoil computes them with NeuralFoil, a neural network trained on XFOIL results, from a NACA 4- or 5-digit designation, a Selig or Lednicer .dat file, or coordinates:

    from pythrust.propeller import AirfoilPolar

naca = AirfoilPolar.from_neuralfoil("NACA 4412")
e387 = AirfoilPolar.from_neuralfoil("e387.dat")
cl, cd = naca.coefficients(alpha_rad=0.1, reynolds=8e4, mach=0.1)
    • NeuralFoil gives the table from -20 to 25 degrees, every 0.5 degree, at 13 Reynolds numbers from 1e4 to 1e6. Beyond -35 and 40 degrees the section is a flat plate, \(c_l = \sin 2\alpha\) and \(c_d = 2 \sin^2 \alpha\). Between the table's points the coefficients are interpolated linearly in the angle of attack and in \(\log Re\); beyond its Reynolds numbers the nearest is used.
    • The angle of attack is from the chord line.
    • Lift is divided by \(\sqrt{1 - M^2}\) (Prandtl-Glauert).
    • n_crit is the amplification factor at which the boundary layer turns turbulent: 9 for a quiet wind tunnel, lower for a turbulent free stream. The default, 7, fits the validation measurements best.
    • Building one table takes about 0.2 s; apc_airfoil_polars() caches its polars per n_crit.

    A blade's sections are all BladeSection or all polars. The analysis stations mix the polars of the two nearest geometry stations linearly. A solved operating point of the APC 10x5E takes about 60 ms.

    API

    Blade Geometry

    pythrust.io.read_qprop_propeller(path) reads a QPROP propeller file, pythrust.io.read_uiuc_geometry(path, diameter_m, section, blade_count=2) a UIUC geometry file, and pythrust.io.read_apc_geometry(path, sections) an APC geometry file into a BladeGeometry, the input of a blade-element propeller model:

    Field Unit Description
    blade_count - Number of blades
    radius_m m Tip radius
    r_m m Station radii, root to tip
    chord_m m Chord at each station
    blade_angle_rad rad Blade angle from the plane of rotation at each station
    sections - BladeSection, AirfoilPolar or BlendedPolar at each station

    BladeSection holds QPROP's airfoil parameters, listed with their symbols in Section lift and drag; AirfoilPolar and BlendedPolar are tabulated polars. section_coefficients(section, alpha_rad, reynolds, mach) evaluates the section model on scalars or NumPy arrays and returns (CL, CD).

    Blade-Element Propeller

    BladeElementPropeller(geometry, mu=1.78e-5, speed_of_sound=340.0, stations=25) is a PropellerModel computed from a BladeGeometry with QPROP's blade-element/vortex formulation:

    from pythrust.io import read_qprop_propeller
from pythrust.propeller import BladeElementPropeller

propeller = BladeElementPropeller(read_qprop_propeller("prop.txt"))
thrust_n, torque_nm = propeller.thrust_torque(rho=1.225, airspeed_mps=10.0, rpm=8000.0)
    Member Description
    geometry The BladeGeometry
    mu, speed_of_sound Air viscosity in kg/(m s) and speed of sound in m/s; the defaults are QPROP's
    diameter_m Twice the tip radius
    max_advance_ratio Advance ratio at which the section at 75% radius has zero lift, an estimate of zero thrust
    thrust_torque(rho, airspeed_mps, rpm) Thrust in N and torque in N m, negative for a windmilling blade; zero at zero RPM, NaN where a station has no solution

    Theory

    Analysis stations

    The blade is described at stations \(r_1 < \dots < r_K\) with chord, blade angle and section parameters. It is analysed at the midpoints of \(N\) equal intervals from \(r_1\) to \(r_K\) (\(N = 25\) by default), each of width \(\Delta r = (r_K - r_1)/N\). Chord, blade angle and section parameters there come from a cubic spline through the stations whose second derivative is constant in the first and last intervals (zero third derivative). The tip radius \(R_{\text{tip}}\) is given, or else \(r_K\).

    Velocities

    Without induced velocity, a station at radius \(r\) sees the axial velocity \(U_a = V\) and the tangential velocity \(U_t = \Omega r\), of magnitude \(U = \sqrt{U_a^2 + U_t^2}\). The velocities with the blade's own induced velocity are written with one angle \(\psi\):

    \[ W_a = \tfrac{1}{2}\left(U_a + U \sin\psi\right) \]
    \[ W_t = \tfrac{1}{2}\left(U_t + U \cos\psi\right) \]

    so that the induced velocities \(v_a = W_a - U_a\) and \(v_t = U_t - W_t\) are perpendicular to the total velocity \(W = \sqrt{W_a^2 + W_t^2}\) for every \(\psi\). At \(\psi_0 = \arctan(U_a / U_t)\) there is no induced velocity. Then

    \[ \phi = \arctan\frac{W_a}{W_t}, \qquad \alpha = \beta - \phi \]
    \[ Re = \frac{\rho W c}{\mu}, \qquad M = \frac{W}{a} \]

    Circulation from the wake

    With the local wake advance ratio \(\lambda_w\) and Prandtl's tip-loss factor \(F_{\text{tip}}\) evaluated on it,

    \[ \lambda_w = \frac{r}{R_{\text{tip}}} \frac{W_a}{W_t} \]
    \[ F_{\text{tip}} = \frac{2}{\pi} \arccos\left( \exp\left[-\frac{B}{2} \left(1 - \frac{r}{R_{\text{tip}}}\right) \frac{1}{\lambda_w}\right] \right) \]

    the circulation of one blade follows from the tangential induced velocity:

    \[ \Gamma_w = v_t \, \frac{4 \pi r}{B} \, F_{\text{tip}} \sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{4 \lambda_w R_{\text{tip}}}{\pi B r}\right)^2} \]

    The square root grows near the hub and keeps the relation consistent with a heavily loaded actuator disk.

    Section lift and drag

    The section's lift is linear in the angle of attack, with the Prandtl-Glauert factor:

    \[ c_{l,\text{lin}} = \frac{c_{l0} + c_{l\alpha}\,\alpha}{\sqrt{1 - M^2}} \]

    Where \(c_{l,\text{lin}}\) passes \(c_{l,\max}\) or \(c_{l,\min}\), the section is stalled and its lift falls off with the angle of attack:

    \[ c_l = \begin{cases} c_{l,\max} \cos\left(\alpha - c_{l0}/c_{l\alpha}\right) & c_{l,\text{lin}} > c_{l,\max} \\ c_{l,\min} \cos\left(\alpha - c_{l0}/c_{l\alpha}\right) & c_{l,\text{lin}} < c_{l,\min} \\ c_{l,\text{lin}} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \]

    The drag is quadratic in the lift and scales with the Reynolds number; a stalled section adds a term that does not:

    \[ c_d = \left[c_{d0} + c_{d2} \left(c_l - c_{l,cd0}\right)^2\right] \left(\frac{Re}{Re_{\text{ref}}}\right)^{e} + \begin{cases} 2 \sin^2\left(\alpha - \alpha_{cd0}\right) & \text{stalled} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \]

    with \(c_{d2} = c_{d2,u}\) for \(c_l > c_{l,cd0}\), \(c_{d2} = c_{d2,l}\) otherwise, and \(\alpha_{cd0} = (c_{l,cd0} - c_{l0})/c_{l\alpha}\). The QPROP guide gives the linear lift, the clipping limits, the drag polar and the stall drag term; the stalled lift and the unscaled stall drag were measured from QPROP 1.22's output.

    Symbol BladeSection field
    \(c_{l0}\), \(c_{l\alpha}\) cl0, cl_alpha_per_rad
    \(c_{l,\min}\), \(c_{l,\max}\) cl_min, cl_max
    \(c_{d0}\), \(c_{d2,u}\), \(c_{d2,l}\), \(c_{l,cd0}\) cd0, cd2_upper, cd2_lower, cl_at_cd0
    \(Re_{\text{ref}}\), \(e\) reynolds_ref, reynolds_exp

    Station equation

    The section's lift gives the circulation \(\Gamma_l = \tfrac{1}{2} W c\, c_l\). A station is solved where the two circulations agree:

    \[ \mathcal{R}(\psi) = \Gamma_w(\psi) - \tfrac{1}{2} W(\psi)\, c\, c_l(\psi) = 0 \]

    \(\psi\) is searched between the angles where \(W_a\) and where \(W_t\) fall to zero, \(-\arcsin(U_a/U) < \psi < \arccos(-U_t/U)\). PyThrust samples \(\mathcal{R}\) on that range, takes the sign change nearest \(\psi_0\), the flow without induced velocity, and refines the root with the Illinois method.

    Thrust and torque

    The lift and drag of a station resolve into thrust and torque along \(\phi\). With \(\Gamma = \tfrac{1}{2} W c\, c_l\) and \(W\cos\phi = W_t\), \(W\sin\phi = W_a\), the midpoint sums over the stations are

    \[ T = \rho B \sum \left(\Gamma W_t - \tfrac{1}{2} W c\, c_d\, W_a\right) \Delta r \]
    \[ Q = \rho B \sum \left(\Gamma W_a + \tfrac{1}{2} W c\, c_d\, W_t\right) r\, \Delta r \]

    At zero RPM the model gives zero thrust and torque; where a station has no root, it gives NaN.

    Start of the RPM search

    The solver starts its RPM search at the advance ratio the propeller model gives as max_advance_ratio. For the blade-element model this is the advance ratio at which the section at 75% radius has zero lift, ignoring the induced velocity, which is small near zero thrust:

    \[ J_{\max} = \pi \, \frac{r_{75}}{R_{\text{tip}}} \tan\left(\beta_{75} - \alpha_{0}\right) \]

    where \(\alpha_0\) is the section's zero-lift angle: \(-c_{l0}/c_{l\alpha}\) for a BladeSection, and for a polar the lowest over its Reynolds numbers, so that the search starts past zero thrust.

    Differences from QPROP

    • Tip radius scaling. A QPROP propeller file may give the tip radius on its second line. The QPROP guide scales it with Rfac and Radd like the station radii; QPROP 1.22 uses it unscaled. PyThrust follows the guide.
    • Multiple roots. At a stalled station of a windmilling blade \(\mathcal{R}(\psi)\) can have more than one root, and QPROP can pick another one than PyThrust.
    • Externally induced velocity. QPROP accepts a velocity induced by another rotor ahead of the propeller; PyThrust does not.

    Verification against QPROP

    The tests compare PyThrust with QPROP 1.22 run on the same input files:

    • Section model: 22 points from \(-68°\) to \(+68°\) angle of attack and Mach 0.13 to 0.55, including both stall limits; \(c_l\) and \(c_d\) agree to the digits QPROP prints.
    • Stations: 7 operating points on two blades, static to negative thrust; \(c_l\), \(c_d\), \(Re\), \(M\) and \(W_a\) at all 25 stations agree to the digits QPROP prints.
    • Thrust and torque: 13 operating points on three blades agree within 0.04%, inside QPROP's four printed digits.
    • Propeller and motor: with a QPROP motor file and a set voltage, RPM, thrust, torque and current agree to the digits QPROP prints.
